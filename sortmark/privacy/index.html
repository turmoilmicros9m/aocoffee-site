Sortmark Privacy Policy

Effective date: 2026-11-08

Sortmark is a Safari extension that saves web pages and sorts them into folders. It is made by Jeffrey Knott. The short version: Sortmark has no developer servers, no accounts and no analytics, and the developer never receives your data.

What Sortmark stores on your device

When you click the Sortmark button (or use its shortcut), Sortmark reads the current page and saves:

  • the page's address, title, description, site name, icon, main image address, type, tags, breadcrumbs, language, image descriptions (alt text), any text you had selected, the address of its feed if the page names one, and the folder it was filed in;
  • on a Mac, a small screenshot (thumbnail) of the part of the page that was visible, only if you turn on "Save page thumbnails" in Settings > Data (off by default on new installs). Thumbnails are never taken in private windows. On iPhone and iPad, Sortmark never takes screenshots;
  • saved copies: if you turn on "Keep a copy of each page you save" in Settings > Preservation (off by default), or choose "Save a copy" in the popup, a readable copy of the page's text and the addresses of its images. It stays on this device only and is never synced or exported. Opening a copy loads its images from the web, so the sites that host them see an ordinary image request;
  • your folders, the sites Sortmark has learned to file for you, the title words and sites it counts when you move a bookmark or confirm its folder (never for a secure folder; kept on this device only, never exported or synced), and your settings;
  • followed feeds and their items: the feeds you follow (up to 100) and the title, address and date of each one's latest posts (up to 50 per feed). They stay on this device only and are never synced or exported.

All of this stays in Safari's storage for the Sortmark extension on your device. If you turn on Sync with iCloud in Settings (it is off by default), your bookmarks, folders and learned sites are also stored in your private iCloud database, which only your Apple Account can access, to keep your devices in sync; thumbnails, API keys and settings are never synced. Nothing is uploaded to the developer or shared with advertisers or data brokers. Thumbnails are never included in exports. Sortmark does not track you.

Sortmark never reads or saves anything from a private window on its own. A page in a private window is saved only if you save it yourself, and then it gets no thumbnail and is never sent to an AI provider.

When Sortmark shows your bookmarks (in the dashboard, for example), Safari loads each saved page's icon and preview image from that page's own site, as it would if you visited the page. Those sites see an ordinary image request; Sortmark adds nothing to it.

Optional: Sync with iCloud

Sync with iCloud is off by default. If you turn it on in Settings > Data on your Macs, iPhones and iPads (signed in to the same Apple Account), Sortmark keeps a copy of your bookmarks, folders and learned sites in your private database in Apple's iCloud (CloudKit), so every device ends up with the same library. Bookmarks already on a device and in iCloud are merged when you turn it on.

  • What syncs: each bookmark's address, title, description, site name, icon (small ones), main image address, folder and dates; your folders; the sites Sortmark learned to file for you; and when you delete them.
  • What never syncs: thumbnails (screenshots), API keys, your Smart sorting choice and consent, appearance and other settings.
  • Where it goes: your own iCloud account, through Apple's CloudKit private database. It counts against your iCloud storage. The developer has no access to it and runs no servers. Page content (address, title, description, icon and folder names) is stored in CloudKit encrypted fields, which are end-to-end encrypted when you turn on Advanced Data Protection for iCloud. The domains of learned sites are part of record names, which are not encrypted.
  • Deletions are remembered for 90 days. If a device hasn't synced for longer than that, bookmarks you deleted elsewhere may come back.
  • To stop: turn off Sync with iCloud in Settings > Data on each device. Your bookmarks stay on the device.
  • To delete the iCloud copy: Settings > Data > "Delete Sortmark's iCloud data…" turns sync off on that device and deletes Sortmark's data from iCloud; your other devices turn sync off the next time they sync and keep their bookmarks. "Delete all data…" turns sync off first and leaves the iCloud copy alone.

Find broken links

Only when you run Find broken links from the dashboard's ⋯ menu does Sortmark contact your saved sites, and it asks then for access to websites unless you already allowed it. Without that access, Sortmark sends one HEAD request, without cookies, to the home page of each saved site to see whether the site still answers. If you allow it, Sortmark instead sends a request, without cookies, to each saved web bookmark's page, without its query string, to read whether the page still exists (and, if that page doesn't answer at all, to the site's home page). For a bookmark that turns out broken, Sortmark also sends one request, without cookies, to its site's home page (https:// + the main domain + /) to see whether that answers, so it can suggest replacing the bookmark with it. Bookmarks in secure folders are left out. Sortmark never reads page content, nothing else is sent, and nothing at all is sent otherwise. The access stays until you revoke it in Settings › Data (you can also turn it off in Safari's extension settings).

Only when you press Find archived copies (or Find archived copy) under Broken links, and after you allow it once, Sortmark sends the address of each broken bookmark, without its query string, to the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine (archive.org) to find an archived copy. Nothing else is sent: no titles, notes, page text or other bookmarks. The archived copy's address and date are kept with the bookmark on this device; they are not synced. Bookmarks in secure folders and on sites you never keep copies of are left out. The Internet Archive's privacy policy applies to those requests.

Only when you press Save my links to the Wayback Machine in Settings › Preservation, and after you allow it once, Sortmark sends the address of each bookmark the archive has no copy of from the last year, without its query string, to the Internet Archive with your own archive.org keys, and the archive makes a public copy of each page. Nothing else is sent. Bookmarks in secure folders, locked or not, and on sites you never keep copies of are left out. Your archive.org keys stay in this browser's extension storage on this device; they are never synced or exported. A bookmark's Save to the Wayback Machine… menu item only opens the archive's own Save page in a new tab.

Followed feeds

Following a site needs the same access to websites. Only when you follow one (Follow in the dashboard or the popup, or Follow a feed… with its address) does Sortmark fetch that site's feed; if it doesn't know the feed's address yet, it first fetches the site's home page and a few common feed addresses on that site to find it. After that, Sortmark fetches only the feeds you follow, only while the dashboard is open (when it opens, in the Feed view, or when you press Refresh), at most once every 30 minutes per feed unless you press Refresh, and never in the background. Each request asks for the feed's address only and is sent without cookies; nothing about you or your bookmarks is sent. The feeds and their posts stay on this device and are not synced or exported. Unfollow (in the Feed view or Settings › Feeds) stops the requests for that feed and removes it and its posts.

Secure folders

You can make a folder a secure folder in Settings. Opening it uses the system's Touch ID, Face ID or password prompt through Apple's LocalAuthentication; Sortmark receives only whether it succeeded, never biometric data. Secure folders are hidden in Sortmark's pages, not encrypted, and they sync like other folders if Sync with iCloud is on. The global switch that hides all secure folders stays on the device where you set it. A folder's own "Hidden while locked" setting syncs with the folder.

Optional: Smart sorting with an AI provider

Smart sorting is off by default. To use it, you add your own API key from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google or xAI in Settings. The switch "Send page details to Anthropic to pick a folder" (it names the provider you selected: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google or xAI) stays off until you save a key. When you turn it on for a provider, Sortmark opens a dialog titled "Allow Anthropic to receive page details?" (again naming that provider) that lists exactly what is sent. Nothing is sent unless you click "Allow" ("Not now" leaves it off), and switching to a different provider asks again. The same list is always shown in Settings under "What Sortmark sends".

Once allowed, whenever Sortmark's built-in rules are unsure where a page belongs, it sends the following to the provider you chose:

  • the page's address without the query string (the part after "?") or "#";
  • the page's title, site name, description, headings, and about the first 600 words of visible text;
  • your list of folder names.

The provider answers with a folder and, unless you turn off "Add a one-line note to bookmarks the AI files or sorts", a one-line note about the page (Smart sort my library also writes one for each bookmark it sorts that has no note); the note is stored with the bookmark on your device, syncs with it if Sync with iCloud is on, and is never sent back to a provider.

Pages that are not http or https are never sent. The request goes directly from Safari to that provider, using your key. It does not pass through the developer. Thumbnails are not sent with a saved page. With Smart sorting on, the request also includes a few of your bookmarks most like the page: their folder, a short title and the site name (never their addresses), so the provider can recognise folders you made yourself; your other bookmarks are not sent. Smart sort my library is different: only when you start it, and after you allow it in its own consent dialog, it sends your bookmark list to the selected provider in batches of 40, up to 2,000 bookmarks. For each bookmark it sends the title, a trimmed address (site and path, without the query string), a trimmed description, the site name, its current folder and whether it is flagged for review, plus your folder names, a few example titles and site names from your folders, and learned site rules; it never sends screenshots, favicon data or your API key. Sort folders, under the same permission and only when you start it, sends your folder names with each one's bookmark count and a few example titles and site names, and the titles and site names of up to 300 bookmarks from your biggest folders, in one request; never their addresses. Page text can include personal information if the page shows it, so you can turn Smart sorting off, or remove your key, at any time. Bookmarks in secure folders, and the names of secure folders, are never sent to an AI provider or included as examples.

The provider handles that information under its own terms and privacy policy:

  • Anthropic: https://www.anthropic.com/privacy
  • OpenAI: https://openai.com/policies/privacy-policy
  • Google: https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • xAI: https://x.ai/legal/privacy-policy

Optional: Smart sorting with Apple Intelligence

On a Mac, iPhone or iPad with Apple Intelligence (macOS 26 or iOS 26 and later), you can pick Apple Intelligence as the provider instead. It needs no key and nothing is sent anywhere: Apple's on-device model runs on your device, inside the Sortmark app, and the page details never leave it, not to Apple and not to the developer. The switch "Use Apple Intelligence to pick a folder" stays off until you turn it on and allow it in a dialog that lists what the model sees: the same details listed above (the page's address without the query string, its title, site name, description, headings, about the first 600 words of visible text, your folder names and a few of your bookmarks most like the page). The model answers with a folder only. Smart sort my library also runs on-device with Apple Intelligence: it goes through your unsure pages and pages in Unsorted one at a time, and nothing is sent anywhere. Duplicates and Sort folders with AI need one of the providers above; Sort folders without AI suggests only merges of similar folder names and removals of empty subfolders.

Your API key

Your key is stored unencrypted in the extension's local storage on this device and is sent only to the provider it belongs to. It is never synced. It is not included in exports. Anyone with access to your user account and Safari's extension storage could read it.

Deleting your data

In Settings you can use "Delete thumbnails…" (Data), "Delete all saved copies…" (Preservation), "Forget all…" (Learned sites, and Learned words), remove your key (Smart sorting), and delete bookmarks. "Delete all data…" (Data, confirmed in the dialog "Delete all Sortmark data?") removes everything Sortmark has stored on that device: bookmarks, thumbnails, saved copies, followed feeds, learned sites and words, folders, settings and keys. After an offline or failed sync, the app's own sync queue on this device may still hold copies until the next sync or until the app is removed. Removing the Sortmark app from a Mac, iPhone or iPad also removes the data Sortmark stored on that device. It does not remove the iCloud copy if you turned on Sync with iCloud: use "Delete Sortmark's iCloud data…" (Settings > Data) before removing the app, or delete Sortmark's data in your device's iCloud storage settings. Data already sent to an AI provider is held by that provider, and you would need to contact them about it.

What we declare on the App Store

In its App Privacy section, Sortmark declares that it collects Browsing History and Other User Content, for App Functionality only, not linked to your identity, and not used for tracking. This covers the optional Smart sorting request described above, including the example titles and site names from your folders. The developer does not receive this data. We declare it because it leaves your device for the provider you chose. Only when you ask, the addresses of broken bookmarks also go to the bookmarked sites themselves (Find broken links) and to the Internet Archive (Find archived copies), and your bookmarks' addresses to the Internet Archive (Save my links to the Wayback Machine); and Sortmark fetches the feeds you follow from their own sites (Followed feeds), as described above.

Where to find this policy

This policy is linked from Settings > About, from the footer of the welcome page that opens after installation, and from the Sortmark app window.

Children

Sortmark is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone.
